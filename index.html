Jie Yuan

Computational Biologist · Machine Learning Scientist · PhD, Computer Science (Columbia)
Arlington, VA · Email

I’m Jie (“Jay”) Yuan, a machine learning scientist and computational biologist with a PhD in Computer Science from Columbia University. I have more than five years of experience designing statistical models and computational pipelines for large-scale biological data.

My work centers on Bayesian modeling, spatial omics, and multi-omics integration. Most recently, in Georg Gerber’s lab at Brigham and Women’s Hospital / Harvard Medical School, I built Bayesian (Stan) pipelines that model C. difficile metabolic dynamics from real-time NMR spectroscopy, along with reproducible multi-omics pipelines for microbiome cohorts. Before that, as a postdoc with Xianjun Dong and Clemens Scherzer at Brigham and Women’s, and then as a research scientist in the Dong lab at the Yale School of Medicine, I led computational development of a multi-omic spatial and temporal cell atlas of Parkinson’s disease, and built machine learning tools for noisy spatial transcriptomics data.

My PhD at Columbia, advised by Itsik Pe’er and Yaniv Erlich, began with DNA.Land, a platform for crowdsourcing genomes and phenomes that grew to over 50,000 users. I co-developed the platform and was co-first author on the paper describing it (Nature Genetics, 2018). I then worked on statistical genetics: polygenic risk scores, heterogeneity in GWAS cohorts, and eQTL associations.

I’m also interested in problems in spatial analysis and geometry, and in interactive data visualization. I care about statistically rigorous experimental design and model evaluation, and about communicating results clearly to collaborators from any background.
