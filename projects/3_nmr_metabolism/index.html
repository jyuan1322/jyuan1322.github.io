Bayesian modeling of C. difficile metabolism

Real-time NMR spectroscopy to metabolic flux, with uncertainty

In the Gerber lab at Brigham and Women’s Hospital / Harvard Medical School, I built preprocessing, calibration, and Bayesian (Stan) modeling pipelines that characterize the metabolic dynamics of Clostridioides difficile from real-time NMR spectroscopy. Concentration trajectories for observed metabolites are modeled as a mixture of logistic functions, and confidence intervals on fluxes are passed as constraint bounds to dynamic flux balance analysis (dFBA, COBRApy) to infer a complete system of metabolic reactions over time. The pipeline cut analysis time from two weeks using conventional tools in TopSpin to six hours.

Analysis pipeline includes processing of raw NMR signals into concentrations, including interactive tools for Lorentzian peak deconvolution and alignment across spectra. Concentration trajectories are modeled as posterior distributions on a mixture of logistic functions.

In the same group, I built reproducible multi-omics pipelines in R and Python for microbiome cohorts (16S, shotgun metagenomics, NMR metabolomics, and proteomics), parallelized on a SLURM cluster. These frameworks allow for fast and professional analysis of new data sets including nested cross-validation and handling of clinical covariates across diverse study designs.
